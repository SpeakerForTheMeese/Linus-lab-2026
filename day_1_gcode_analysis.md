(1) In your gcode_rect_pocket.nc, what is the purpose of leaving 0.3mm stock on the walls during roughing? Why not cut to final dimension in the roughing pass?
    Leaving stock on the walls in the roughing pass allows you to use a larger tool during first passes, and therefore remove more material and finish the part faster. The larger diameter tool you use, the more material you can remove per pass. This comes with a tradeoff, however, as larger tools are unable to fit into corners, or properly define tight corners. The use of a second pass with a smaller tool allows for close finishing, and speeds can be sped down to improve accuracy. 
(2) Your pre-lab correctly said verify the WCS before cutting — what is the consequence of the WCS being offset by +5mm in X? Trace through your G-code to show which specific move would go wrong.
    An offset in WCS would affect the entire part, moving every action over by a set off set. for the rectangular pocket and the boss, this would only result in the feature being offset in the part, but in the contour, a +5 mm offset in WCS would result in a cut being made completly off the material, which could potentially damage clamps and the spindle. An x-offset would scrap the part, but is less damaging that a Z-offset would be, as that would likely damage the spindle or the clamps in the machine. 
(3) From your cutting_parameters.py output, why does a larger tool diameter require a lower spindle RPM to achieve the same surface cutting speed?
    Cutting speed is not determined by rpm, but rather by the speed the tool surface is moving past the material, so a tool with a larger diameter will need a lower cutting speed to achieve the samle velocity at the circumference.
